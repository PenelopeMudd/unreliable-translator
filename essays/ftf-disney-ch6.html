Warner Day 4: Andretti’s

When we last left our family, the parents had returned from the 7:45 PM meeting feeling seen and connected — and everyone looked forward to seeing each other in the morning at Andretti’s.

By Wednesday, the itinerary changed its gait. After three full days of parks in the Florida sun, we went off the Disney property altogether, toward Andretti Indoor Karting & Games, a family entertainment center. The morning still began at 8:30, but the day it opened onto was a softer one as we had the place to ourselves for go-karts, laser-tag, arcade games, and pin bowling, with brunch to follow. Even the packing list had readied us for it — closed-toe shoes with socks, long hair pulled back for racing — small instructions that meant no family arrived unprepared for a day that looked nothing like the ones before.

Groups had their schedules booked for racing and laser-tag with time for games as they wanted. My family ended up riding go-karts in the same round as Kurt and his son-in-law. I ended up in third place — Kurt got first in our group.

After a few hours, we all gathered for pin-bowling. Families played together with volunteers and everyone cheered each other on. Some of the older kids and volunteers had quite the rivalry going on about who was or was not winning at the competition — the current one being bowling. For me, it felt like a great day at an indoor summer camp where you can’t really tell who is “in-charge” of whom.

Once all the games were played and championship bragging rights decided — for the moment — we went in to have brunch. After a few words from Kurt, one of the “Wish Kids” offered a blessing to the meal. We soon were all happily eating, again, as food appeared and volunteers checked to see that each family’s drinks were full and napkins were present.

As the morning began to wind down, people talked about plans for the open afternoon. Soon Kurt called us all together again, thanked us for coming, and reminded everyone that it was Ms. Brenda’s actual birthday that day, and we all joyously cheered and sang with Kurt beaming from the front of the room. Next, one of the boys brought over a card for Brenda that all the kids had signed. She was openly touched, and he offered a hug. Pretty soon, a room full of “Wish Kids,” Warner grandkids, and any other kids in the room jumped up to hug Ms. Brenda for her birthday.
