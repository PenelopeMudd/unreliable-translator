Warner Day 3: Hollywood Studios, Pt. 2

When we last left our family, Kim and Zack Warner had spent the morning together at Hollywood Studios — Toy Story, weather radio updates, and a sweat-soaked elbow neither of them mentioned.

After the Toy Story morning, including the Slinky Dog coaster—which I wasn’t sure was “chill” but Zack assured me that we were still within those boundaries—the Thrill Seekers left to find all things Star Wars, and I ended up back with Sierra and my grandchildren for our next adventures before lunch. During this time of sing-alongs, I learned more about the evening’s events. According to the itinerary:

7:45 PM — ⭐ Parents Only Meeting with Kurt and Brenda

Upstairs conference room, Towne Hall at Give Kids the World.

Grandparents/volunteers watch kids.

Don’t miss this one.

I told our volunteer, Sierra, that I would be at the villa if the kids changed their minds about going with her, and I felt like we had a good plan that would allow my daughter and her husband to be present at the meeting without concern for who had the kids. Soon it was time to gather both groups at our waiting vans for transport to lunch which was—in fact—sandwiches at a beautiful resort. At the end of the meal, Kurt got everyone’s attention with cake and approached our table. He announced to the room that it was my Avery’s birthday and we ended our lunch by all singing with Kurt leading the song.

Early that afternoon, we were headed back to Give Kids the World Village for more slushies and popcorn by the pool. This was the third Disney day, and families had their meeting at 7:45 pm, so we lounged around and took advantage of the downtime. After dinner, both grandchildren said with no hesitation that they would like to go with Sierra as she was taking them to the Candyland park on the Village campus. My “kids” were off to their meeting, so I snuggled into my twin bed to read. After a while, both little ones burst back into the villa, all smiles, with their volunteer in tow. I thanked her, and we looked forward to seeing each other in the morning at Andretti’s, a family entertainment center.

Soon, Randy and Jordan returned from the parents’ meeting eager to fill me in on the experience. They described a gathering marked by in-person, human interaction, where life’s challenges were shared openly without judgment. Historically, Brenda and Kurt have been upfront about their parenting challenges and Zack’s needs; at this event, according to my daughter, all participants gathered around a conference table to honor each family’s experience. However, I was not present in this space and will leave that story to those who were. What I did observe was its effect. My whole family returned from this evening feeling seen, connected, and entrusted with carrying those connections into the rest of the week’s celebration.
