Warner Disney Adventure: Reflection

When invited to celebrate Avery Dees, cancer butt-kicker, with my daughter and her family at Disney, I did not set out to conduct an investigation of the Warner family. I accepted a gift. Because of who I am by training as well as curiosity, I started asking questions. In a search for these answers, I Googled Warners, read their books and found out that Kurt WAS on Dancing with the Stars as I had supposed. Also, I now theorize he became motivated to learn to dance because Ms. Brenda is often the hottest thing on any dance floor, red cowboy boots or not.

As I looked at First Things First and followed them on social media where the Warners talked about Zack’s life and their dedication to his happiness. Before we ever sat on Slinky Dog, I read about Treasure House where Zack lives. It wasn’t until after meeting the Warners in-person and seeing the care they give to all the Wish Kids and their families that I witnessed Treasure House’s mission live without ever stepping in Arizona.

However, warm and fuzzies aside, the investigation was not over, and as a fellow Midwesterner, I think that the Warners would insist for the reader and this researcher-in-Mimi’s-clothing to take them at their own words.

In 2000, in front of the nation, Kurt said “First things first…” Repeatedly he has referenced that this statement refers to Matthew 6:33: “But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.” On our final night, Kurt stood in front of us all and talked to all of us: Wish Kids, Warners, Families of Wish Kids, and he reminded us that the “devil” can get in our path but that we have to be that light. We cannot quit because each person in that room is worth fighting for, no matter what brought us to that point or where we might head from there.

While Kurt and Brenda seem to work as a unified front on all things, Brenda’s certainty has a source.

“I am the mother of a child that deserves to be seen, deserves to be heard, deserves to be enjoyed, and deserves a full life.”
— Brenda Warner  /  treasurehouse.org

Many chapters ago, I wanted to know something about the founders of First Things First. Not whether they were generous; the evidence for that was already sitting in my inbox in the form of their family trip to Disney World followed by a slew of gifts that would just show up at our villa nearly daily. (I mean really—how crazy is that?! I can hear my mom saying, “Kim Marie, why can’t you just send a thank you card.”) Anywho…

I wanted to illustrate what care looks like, so I looked to that revered attachment: The official Warner Disney Itinerary. If you’ve ever been on one of “those” family vacations you know that itineraries can easily become binding contracts especially to those of us who are more creative with our interpretations of arrival times and deadlines.

Since formal documents with group expectations give me the willies, I warily spelunked the depths of this powerful text. With the mighty power of “Intended Order from Chaos,” I looked to see what sort of agreement the Warners had proposed in this foundational document.

When read from this vantage:

Participants were promised:

  1. Five days together with Kurt, Brenda, and their family—all four Disney parks, Andretti karting and games, VIP access and guides, daily meals, fireworks and dessert, photographs, and autographs.
  2. Communication—daily previews from Kurt, plus direct access to Jen Zink and each family’s volunteer throughout the trip.
  3. Accessibility—different options and accommodations built into the plans so families could participate according to their own needs rather than everyone being expected to do the same thing.
  4. Human connection—the trip was framed from the beginning as more than five days of activities, with the stated hope of creating “bonds that last well beyond the trip.”

Warners requested:

  1. Do: If you choose to participate, be where you need to be when you need to be, and follow the practical instructions for that activity.
  2. Please do: Join them for the Tuesday parents-only meeting and Thursday group photo, wearing the provided T-shirt for the photo.

Let’s just say that if this magical itinerary of dates and times were boiled down to its essence, it would contain the promise of this gift: Time with the Warner Family, Communication, Accessibility, and Human Connection. These were fulfilled among games of tag and below fireworks with smiles, giggles, cheers, hugs, and tears—all washed down with ice cold water and popsicles delivered by the volunteers to ease the Florida sun.

The columns in our document could list the Warner kids’ names, but not who loves Star Wars, who has plans in LA, and who has serious motion sickness. No sheet told me Sierra would work all week to earn a shy little guy’s hand while balancing the attentions of five-year-old Princess Finley, who started bellowing every time a box arrived at the Villa, “Mom! Mimi? Is this from Kurt Warner?”

Although lists, checkboxes, and expectations in emails can offer cohesive communication, they can’t give an NFL Superstar the inspiration to sing for a Wish Kid on his birthday and to get a room full of strangers to enthusiastically sing along.

Most importantly, no line item covered the imperative nature of a weather radio’s batteries in contrast to the inconsequential nature of a sweaty elbow on new friendships.

If you wonder what it takes to create a magical experience of healing for fifty strangers on your family vacation, learn about how First Things First developed their itinerary, and then watch closely how Brenda Warner looks at Zack. Mix that together and try to translate that devotion to every child you can impact. Finally, take that recipe, and repeat it for twenty-four years or so.

On the last day of our Warner Disney Adventure we each got a copy of Zack’s card. I’ll let him and his mom illustrate what it means to be present, to communicate, and to connect with dignity.
