Warner Day 5: Celebration

When we last left our family, brunch at Andretti’s ended with a room full of children jumping up to hug Ms. Brenda for her birthday — and everyone headed home looking forward to one more day together.

Thursday, in our Warner Family Trip matching T-shirts, we headed out the door of our Give Kids the World villa for Disney. Our first event was a 3:30 “Worship service led by Kurt and Brenda.” After that there was time for dinner, autographs, group photos, and then we were on to the Magic Kingdom. For someone from my heritage, the words “Worship Service” can evoke a variety of expectations. I was curious to see how the afternoon would align with the experience we had shared thus far.

When we walked from the darkened hotel hall, there was a space much like a ballroom with unadorned beige walls, bigger than we had seen before for our meetings. A photo shoot set up for the later group picture was at the room’s entrance. To either side of the photo area, there was a table for autographs Kurt and Brenda would sign, and an area where the buffet would appear after our service. In the center of the stark room stood three rows of chairs — enough for our party of forty to fifty. After the day’s greetings, all the families moved to their seats to hear Kurt and Brenda’s message.

Although I was not at the parent meeting, I have to think that it must have felt very similar to the experience I had that night in the Contemporary. First of all, Kurt got up and talked about life’s difficulties. He used the example of your “light and the bushel,” which was familiar to most of us in the room. As he looked at each person throughout his talk, he reminded us that we can’t give up even when life gets most challenging. Next, he thanked us for being there and sharing this experience with his family. Then, the volunteers all came up and shared what the week meant to them. They spoke without notes — with names and specific examples that were meaningful to each one of them. By this point, all the names meant something to our family as well. We could appreciate the stories they told because we were part of the process. We knew who rode rides together, and who was likely to instigate a game of tag.

Sierra, the “best Warner,” got up to share how she worked so hard to get my grandson to hold her hand in the park, and when he finally did, how happy she was that he gave in! This was true. Avery takes time to warm up, so a hand, much less the unsolicited hug he offered Ms. Brenda, are the gold stars of comfort in his world. In this typical event space, down a nondescript hotel hallway, the Warners took some banquet chairs and created a place to see and be seen for anyone who was willing to come along.

As the gathering ended, families drifted toward tables for dinner. Soon each group gathered one at a time for autographs while the last evening’s dinner was served. Although we rode rides, watched the Disney parade from a private balcony, and ate popsicles while the most awe-inspiring fireworks lit up Cinderella’s Castle, the whole magical experience was over in a blur that night. As the lights returned and we all gathered our belongings to head to our cars, people laughed, tears flowed, and Kurt Warner walked around to personally hug every person in the party and to thank them for being there — encouraging them again to keep in touch.
