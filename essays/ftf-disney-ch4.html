Warner Day 3: Hollywood Studios, Pt. 1

When we last left our family, Kurt had announced it was “Ms. Brenda’s birthday week” to the entire Rainforest Cafe, and the group headed back to Give Kids the World Village for an afternoon of slushies and ice cream by the pool.

After meeting with our group on Tuesday, we were headed into Hollywood Studios. As most often happens when previous strangers have repeated positive interactions, people greeted each other from various family groups and talked about the day’s plans while kids chased and ran in circles heedless of the morning heat. For many of the adults — families and volunteers alike — today was Star Wars day where they would get to experience some of their favorite fictional worlds come to life, so anticipation was high all around. We would spend the first part of the morning together, as per the plan; then we would break off so that the Thrill Seekers could spend the rest of their time among the Empire. I, on the other hand, noticed that Zack was walking with his brother, Kade, a true Star Wars fan, so this might be an opportunity to get to spend time with Zack.

Since reading Brenda’s book, One Call Away: A Memoir, I had a picture of the circumstances of Brenda’s early life as a mother. Having tried single-motherhood myself in the 1990s, I related to her struggles to caretake, find employment, and hold together a crumbling unit by sheer will alone. Truly, I was entranced by how I, a less-than-devout-non-conformist, could connect so to a former die-hard Marine, self-professed Jesus Freak, and now wealthy celebrity. However, I know what it is to fight to protect those who you chose to bring to this planet, and on that note, Brenda Warner and I were of one mind.

Brenda’s public advocacy for Zack came on our first evening as she made their introductions from the front of the banquet room. She said that Zack likes to “walk with married ladies” through the park and told us how to hold our right arm in case we wanted to offer to visit with him. She reminded us that since his accident Zack does not see well and that sometimes he needs his walking partner to move more slowly. Before we ever caught sight of even one of Mickey’s ears, I was eager to learn more about Zachary Warner, the source of Brenda Warner’s devotion, and now I had my chance!

As I said, I noticed that Zack was walking with his brother Kade. Since they didn’t seem to have anything important to discuss, my grandchildren were happily connected to our volunteer and their own parents, and I was, in fact, a “married lady” who very much liked to walk and talk, I approached them. Soon, Zack and I were headed off among the group into Hollywood Studios with him holding onto my right arm.

At first, all I could think was, “Move slow. Where are we supposed to be? Am I doing this right? What if I steer us into danger and break Brenda Warner’s precious son?” As a life-long teacher, I am trained to meet other’s needs, but my classroom had never been quite so open and mobile with a celebrity pupil. Pretty soon, after we discussed some of Zack’s favorite subjects, including weather radios and regional popular music stations, I realized that I was overthinking things. Ms. Brenda said to lead and give Zack my right arm. I was doing that. I would do my job, and I would let Zack do his.

As we smiled and chatted our way through Toy Story, I learned a lot about the rides and events we would encounter. You see, this was Zack’s 24th year on this First Things First trip, so he knew the whole day. Moving through the queues, he would let me know when interactions might be more intense or when they were calmer. Also, I learned his opinion on the day’s lunch — sandwiches — Zack was not a fan, but he let me know that the venues were able to find something he preferred over those choices, so he did not seem concerned about it.

Just like in Beauty and the Beast, plaid-adorned guides shepherded us between attractions, cold bottled waters appeared from volunteers to quench the heat, and I learned more about Zack Warner. On this day, his weather radio only had one bar — an important distinction — as being prepared for the weather is imperative to Zack. He assured me that his family was going to take care of the battery problem when they returned to their rental home, and when Kurt walked by us, he reiterated to Zack that batteries were definitely on the list for that evening. Soon, I learned about Treasure House, where Zack lives in Arizona, and his plans to travel to a friend’s house, an annual post-Disney tradition, where he was already aware of the regional weather band station. Overall, my greatest sense of distress was the sweat-soaked quality of the elbow Zack was holding, but he seemed not one bit bothered, so we proceeded through our morning, just two new acquaintances on a great Hollywood Studios adventure among forty other friends on group vacation.
